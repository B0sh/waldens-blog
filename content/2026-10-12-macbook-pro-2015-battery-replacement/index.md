Huge win for my [self-repair](/tags/self-repair) journey. I did my first MacBook battery replacement!

This is for the [early 2015 retina MacBook Pro 13in](https://everymac.com/systems/apple/macbook_pro/specs/macbook-pro-core-i5-2.7-13-early-2015-retina-display-specs.html) model. I'd like to share some pictures and how the job went for me, but if you found this blog hoping for a tutorial on the 2015 MacBook Pro battery replacement you're out of luck. You should follow the [iFixit Guide](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/MacBook+Pro+13-Inch+Retina+Display+Early+2015+Battery+Replacement/45137) like I did. I didn't substantially use any other resources.

This my first attempted "Difficult" rating on iFixit, presumably due to the fact that the entire battery component is glued on and the general delicate nature of Mac internal parts.

---

### Before

I pointed out to my friend: "woah dude your laptop is totally bulging" 

That means the battery has swollen and needs to be replaced, so I asked if I could try it myself. (Well, after I tried to get him to just get a new one!)

Before doing the repair and getting the parts I read through the entire [guide](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/MacBook+Pro+13-Inch+Retina+Display+Early+2015+Battery+Replacement/45137) and read about safety when dealing with swollen batteries. (Not covered here, do your own research!)

### Disassembly

I could immediately feel the swollen batteries when I started disassembly. I tried to keep the back case held down with my arm so the screws wouldn't strip, although I don't know how possible that is. Considering how the batteries look underneath it's remarkable the case didn't warp more. There was so much pressure under here.

Next was disconnecting the battery and surrounding parts. 

I got a vibe that maybe this computer had been worked on once before but I'm not sure. The was a lot of flux and large solder blobs around the existing battery control board. (By the way, learning soldering is on my todo list!) 

Being able to quickly mouse over between different photos on iFixit's repair guide was actually so nice, it helped me visualize exactly the movement I was supposed to be doing. I really didn't want to break any of the logic board connectors.

### The Repair

The iOpener is a alternative to a heatgun, for helping break down the glue in products. You can throw it in the microwave to heat it, and just set it down where you need to apply heat. My only issue was how the guide presented it like a minute of heat would be good enough to loosen the glue, but for me it was more like 10 minutes and several reheatings - and I still had to use a more force then I wanted to remove the adhesive. The main thing with swollen batteries I read is that puncturing them is very bad, so I used the recommended plastic cards to prevent damage.

Putting in the new battery was easily the scariest part. You have one shot to get the alignment correct, and despite my best efforts, I was off ever so slightly. I was forced to bend the battery connector a bit to make it work. I have no idea what these little rubber stoppers are doing here at the bottom either.

### Reflections

It took me 2 hours to do this repair, but I went slow and double checked everything I was doing. I think I pushed my comfort zone out ever so slightly with this as I've never worked with any kind of repair involving adhesive. 

I'm surprised that the case just went back to its original non-bended shape instantly. I was worried there'd be permanent damage there but it wasn't an issue.

Most of all though I'm happy that this computer which could've easily been pitched to the side gets another chance to be useful!
